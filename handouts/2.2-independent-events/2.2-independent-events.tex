\prob Suppose that a fair coin is tossed twice. Let $A$ be the event that the second toss is a head, and $B$ the event that the first toss is a head. Compute $P(A\mid B)$. What do you notice?

\vfill
\prob (Example 2.2.3) Suppose that a balanced die is rolled. Let $A$ be the event that an even number is obtained, and let $B$ be the event that one of the numbers $1$, $2$, $3$, or $4$ is obtained. Show that the events $A$ and $B$ are independent.

\vfill
\newpage
\prob (Example 2.2.4) Suppose that a fair coin is tossed twice. Let $A$ be the event that the first toss is a head, $B$ the event that the second toss is a head, and $C$ the event that both tosses are the same. Are $A$, $B$, and $C$ independent?

\vfill
\prob Let $D$ be the event that you are sick with a disease, $T$ the event that a test for the disease shows positive, and $V$ the event that you go on vacation. You are given the following information:

\medskip
\begin{itemize}
\item The probability that you have the disease, the test shows positive, and you go on vacation is $0.144$.
\item The probability that you have the disease and the test shows positive is $0.72$.
\item The probability that the test shows positive and you go on vacation is $0.156$.
\item The probability that the test shows positive is $0.78$.
\end{itemize}

Are the events $D$ that you have the disease and $V$ that you go on vacation conditionally independent given the event $T$ that the test shows positive? Does your answer make sense intuitively?
